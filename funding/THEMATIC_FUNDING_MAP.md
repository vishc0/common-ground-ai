# Thematic Funding Map

## One platform, many public-interest applications

Common Ground AI is horizontal civic and knowledge infrastructure. Funding proposals should not claim that the whole platform is an education app, workforce app, health app, or climate app. Instead, each proposal funds a bounded pilot that exercises the same core system in one context.

```text
Core platform
  ├─ safe contextual question intake
  ├─ structured AI proposal and three levels of “why”
  ├─ agreement, disagreement, alternatives, and outcomes
  ├─ evidence, moderation, and appeals
  └─ Tree of Life knowledge relationships
        ├─ education pilot
        ├─ workforce pilot
        ├─ civic/community information pilot
        ├─ climate resilience pilot
        ├─ accessibility pilot
        ├─ household/economic security pilot
        └─ public-health navigation pilot (later; higher safeguards)
```

## Shared funder interests

| Interest | Common Ground contribution | Example low-risk pilot | Evidence to measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Responsible and trustworthy AI | Inspectable rationales, uncertainty, dissent, appeals, provenance, outcome feedback | Compare majority voting with evidence- and cohort-aware evaluation | Correction rate, appeal outcomes, dissent preservation, harmful-answer rate |
| Community power and participation | Communities define problems and challenge links/conclusions | Neighborhood problem-solving cohort | Participation diversity, accepted challenges, decisions influenced by community |
| Education and AI literacy | Learn to ask, evaluate, verify, disagree, and improve—not merely consume answers | Adult/community learning or disadvantaged learners | Critical-evaluation gains, source verification, useful action rate |
| Labor and economic mobility | Capture worker-defined problems and context-sensitive solutions | Shift workers, job transitions, or small-business operations | Time saved, outcome adoption, worker control, subgroup parity |
| Civic information and democracy | Organize evidence and principled disagreement without engagement ranking | Local service or community-information questions | Information usefulness, correction speed, viewpoint coverage |
| Digital rights and privacy | Coarse optional demographics, pseudonymous UID, deletion, minimum data | Privacy-preserving community research | Data collected per participant, deletion completion, PII incident rate |
| Accessibility and disability inclusion | Adapt format/language and expose where “universal” answers fail | Accessible public-service navigation | Completion rate, WCAG testing, outcome parity, user-led corrections |
| Climate and community resilience | Reuse context-specific local solutions while exposing constraints | Heat preparedness, home efficiency, emergency readiness | Preparedness actions, local relevance, avoided duplication |
| Household and economic security | Practical shared learning around costs, maintenance, care, and resources | Emergency repairs or household budgeting | Useful-action rate, avoided cost, safety referrals |
| Public health navigation | Compare resource-navigation approaches without diagnosing | Finding non-emergency community resources | Successful referrals, misinformation rate, escalation safety |
| Open knowledge and public digital infrastructure | Open schemas, portable data, transparent governance, reusable components | Publish taxonomy and evaluation toolkit | Reuse, contributors, independent audits, interoperable exports |

## Funding-fit rules

A funding opportunity is a fit when all are true:

1. It funds at least one shared interest above.
2. The proposed pilot uses the existing core architecture rather than creating a bespoke product.
3. The funder accepts independent evaluation and publication of limitations.
4. No participant-level data, ranking influence, exclusivity, or favorable conclusions are exchanged.
5. The pilot can meet the required safety level and be stopped safely.
6. Restricted work leaves reusable value for the broader public platform.

Reject or redesign an opportunity when it requires surveillance, targeted persuasion, identity reconstruction, political campaigning, discriminatory profiling, opaque moderation, sponsor-controlled conclusions, or lock-in that prevents data/code portability.

## Thematic proposal formula

> **Shared problem:** `[community]` experiences `[measurable problem]`, and existing AI/forums fail because `[context, trust, or evidence gap]`.
>
> **Pilot:** Common Ground AI will work with `[community partner]` to run a `[duration]` pilot focused on `[topic]`, using the same privacy-preserving question, answer, disagreement, outcome, and knowledge-graph infrastructure.
>
> **Funder alignment:** This advances `[funder interest]` by `[specific mechanism]`.
>
> **Public value:** The pilot will leave behind `[open schema/tool/evaluation/report]` reusable across other topics.
>
> **Measurement:** We will measure `[three outcome metrics]` and publish limitations, safety incidents, and subgroup differences.

## Pilot sequencing by risk

### Start first

- adult/community learning;
- household organization and non-emergency maintenance;
- local climate preparedness;
- workplace coordination that does not make employment decisions;
- civic/service information from authoritative public sources.

### Add after moderation and expert partnerships

- benefits navigation;
- financial education without individualized financial advice;
- public-health resource navigation without diagnosis;
- youth participation with age-appropriate consent and safeguards;
- housing/legal information with qualified referral paths.

### Do not fund as an early pilot

- diagnosis or treatment recommendations;
- legal determinations;
- credit, employment, insurance, housing, or benefits eligibility decisions;
- political persuasion or voter targeting;
- law-enforcement or immigration risk scoring;
- systems intended to identify anonymous participants.

## Portfolio allocation target

- 50% unrestricted/core infrastructure and safety
- 25% themed community pilots
- 15% independent evaluation, security, and governance
- 10% reserve and exploration

Avoid a portfolio where one restricted funder supplies more than 35% of annual operating resources unless governance protections and an exit plan are in place.
